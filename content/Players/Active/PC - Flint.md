Flint arrived in [[PC - Needleberry]] after becoming lost on the winter roads of Southern Aedryn. He was found by [[PC - Vadim]], who recognised the approaching blizzard and guided him towards the settlement. Upon arriving, Flint received assistance from Bromm Hartwell, who repaired part of his damaged frame.

When the creatures attacked [[PC - The Bent Needle]], Flint joined Dean, Ash and Vadim in pursuing them through the town. Together, they fought and killed one using alchemist's fire.

During the investigation that followed, Flint displayed an unexplained connection to the creatures. When Vadim lost their trail beneath the snow, Flint unknowingly continued leading the party in the correct direction. This brought them to [[PC - The Cave]] outside Needleberry.

Inside the cave, an unseen voice referred to a Custodian shortly before the creatures awakened and attacked. Flint escaped alongside the others and helped bring down one of the creatures trapped within the upper chamber.

After returning to Needleberry, Flint initially sided with Vadim during the disagreement with Irilard Belcons. He later agreed to the party remaining together and helping draw the creatures away from the Town Hall.

During the defence of [[PC - Belcons Manor]], Flint fought the creatures within the generator room. Although temporarily brought down, he recovered and killed one of them with his blade before helping the party escape to the fortified dining hall.

Following Belcons' death, Flint suffered a violent malfunction. His body became unresponsive while his core began glowing red. Although his systems remained functional, something within his mind appeared to be forcing him into a shutdown state.

While unconscious, Flint experienced a series of fragmented memories. He saw a laboratory containing countless reflections of himself and an iron door asking him who he was. Other fragments showed him aboard a familiar ship, travelling alone through the wilderness and working within a containment facility filled with creatures resembling those attacking Needleberry.

Within the oldest of these fragments, a dwarven figure addressed Flint as **Custodian** and ordered him to deal with a hostile specimen.

Flint eventually awakened and spoke three words:

**"Kadun, Barum, Vornak."**

The command caused all the creatures, including the red-eyed one, to freeze. Although the red-eyed creature later resisted further commands, it hesitated when confronted by Flint and eventually declared:

**"Custodian authority recognised. Fault in mission parameters. Returning Home."**

The creature then abandoned the battle.

Flint's exact relationship with the creatures remains unknown, but they recognised his authority and identified him as a Custodian.

When the party returned to the cave, Flint experienced further fragments showing that it had once served as a dwarven camp. By concentrating upon the connection he felt towards the escaped creature, he determined that it had travelled towards the [[PC - The Stoneward Spine]].

Before leaving Needleberry, Bromm gifted Flint a custom-made silver hook weapon. Bromm also advised him to find the Redenten living within the mountains, believing their technology might be able to help him.

Flint departed Needleberry alongside Dean and Ash, travelling towards the Stonewood Spine in pursuit of the red-eyed creature and answers concerning his forgotten identity.

During the journey north, Flint reacted automatically when addressed with certain Dwarven commands. He declared that his obedience protocols had been disengaged, although he appeared to have little understanding of the response.

[[PC - Aleksander]] recognised similarities between Flint's behaviour and the supernatural control exerted by powerful vampires.

Ash attempted to investigate Flint's internal mechanisms. Opening one of Flint's rear panels triggered a violent electrical discharge that knocked Ash unconscious and warped the tool in his hand.

Shortly afterwards, Flint stood rigidly upright, his eyes turned red and he began speaking in harsh Dwarven. The event ended as suddenly as it began, and Flint appeared to have no memory of what occurred.

The origin and purpose of these responses remain unknown.
